Structural Analysis

Did Per Capita GDP Grow in Accordance with the Growth Story?

Short Verdict: Mathematically yes, but structurally no.

1. The Proportional Convergence (Mathematical Illusion)

On paper, Per Capita Income moves in near-perfect symmetry with total real GDP growth. During the 2006–2010 boom (GDP 8.0%), Per Capita growth peaked at 6.5%. During the 2016–2020 deceleration (GDP 5.5%), Per Capita growth dropped to 4.3%.

Demographic Factor: India's population growth slowed from 2.1% in 1991 to under 0.9% by 2026. This baseline compression mathematically inflates the average per capita output even when median wages stall.
2. The Structural Disconnection (Distributional Reality)

Per Capita GDP is a simple arithmetic mean ($\text{Total GDP} \div \text{Population}$). It assumes uniform distribution. In reality, India Inc and stock market gains concentrated heavily among the top 10%, while real rural wages stayed flat.

Public Service Squeeze: Chronic underfunding of Education (~3% GDP) and Health (~1.4% GDP) forced the bottom 80% to pay private out-of-pocket costs, eroding gains in material living standards.
